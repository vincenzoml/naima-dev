Finding (long work, specification settled): the wait loops of 2026-10-07 — 13 self-matching pgrep -f loops running up to 10.5 h, ssh polls of a hung remote run, a measurement server's disk filled to 100% — share one cause: agents improvised how to wait, how to tell a process alive, and what to clean, and nothing bounded any of it. The specification of the long-work toolset (specs/long-work-naima-run-wait-run-list) answers each with a bound by construction: every run has a time budget with no default, a disk budget on what it declares it creates, liveness from a supervisor's heartbeat rather than the process table, a wait that always ends, and a rule shipped with Naima that makes these the only way; nine requirements, each with the test that will prove it and the part of the incident it answers.
